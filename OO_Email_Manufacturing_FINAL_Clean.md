# WHY THESE CHANGES — for review

## The brief this was written against

From the Outlook Orange × Sendō bi-monthly followup on 19 October, and from Dylan and Bonita's emails that week:

1. **Bonita flagged AI fatigue** in the market. Buyers are tired of outreach that reads as machine-written, and it damages the sender.
2. **"Human-first" narrative** was agreed as the direction for upcoming outreach.
3. **"Commodity to brand"** is the core transformation story for industrial targets.
4. **Dylan committed case studies** — NUVO Rubber, Continental Cables, Intellisec — to be worked into the messaging.
5. **Antoine's note:** the client wants to focus on B2B. How do you build a strong brand even in B2B?

## What was making the old copy read as AI-written

These are the specific patterns that give machine-written copy away, all of which were present:

| Pattern | Old copy | Why it reads as AI |
|---|---|---|
| **Colon + three-item list** | "shows up in the numbers: slower sales cycles, weaker repeat orders, and more pressure on pricing" | The single most recognisable AI sentence shape. Appeared twice. |
| **Four-item list in the closer** | "a leadership change, entering a new market, a new growth phase, or a sense the story..." | Same pattern, longer. |
| **Abstract nouns doing the work** | alignment, positioning, perception, narrative, equity | Humans name things. Machines describe categories. |
| **Even sentence rhythm** | Every sentence 15–25 words | Real writing lurches. Some sentences are four words long. |
| **Polished aphorisms** | "that gap quietly costs orders", "real brand equity isn't built in a single campaign" | Reads like a LinkedIn caption, not a person. |
| **Symmetrical CTA** | "Would you be open to a quick chat if any of that sounds familiar?" | Over-hedged and over-balanced. People ask shorter questions. |
| **No specifics** | No names, places, numbers or concrete detail anywhere except NUVO | Specificity is the hardest thing for a machine to fake and the fastest signal of a real sender. |

## What was done instead

**Sentence length now varies hard.** "The business has moved on. The brand hasn't quite caught up." Four and seven words. Then a longer one. That lurch is what human writing sounds like.

**Lists were dismantled.** No colon-lists anywhere. Email 4's four triggers became three fragments of uneven length, which is how people actually speak.

**Concrete replaced abstract.** "The quotes you lose on price" instead of "slower sales cycles." "The supplier down the road" instead of "competitors." "Walked a factory floor" instead of "people who understand the business."

**A real opinion was added.** "Plenty of agencies can redraw a logo." That is a position, and machines avoid positions.

**Email 3 now names the elephant.** "You've probably had a dozen emails like mine this week, and most of them weren't written by a person." Acknowledging it directly is disarming, and it is the only credible way to make an anti-AI argument inside a cold email. This is the email that most directly answers Bonita's concern.

**The closer stopped selling.** "I'd rather you had my name already than went looking for someone" gives the prospect a graceful exit, which is what makes people reply.

**Every P.S. is different.** The live campaign runs the same "Not yet" P.S. on three emails. Identical repeated blocks are themselves an automation signal. Each now does a different job: Email 1 offers an exit, Email 2 offers value with no call attached, Email 3 invites correction.

## What stayed

The sequence logic from V2 is unchanged and was sound:

- **E1** the gap · **E2** the proof · **E3** the differentiator · **E4** the graceful close

NUVO Rubber remains the proof point. The AI angle remains the differentiator. Only the execution changed.
